Tilden and Wildcat Canyon: Two Berkeley Hills Parks, One Easy Weekend

The hills above Berkeley and Richmond hold two neighboring East Bay Regional Park District parks that suit very different moods. One is a classic family park with a carousel and a swimming lake. The other is a quieter stretch of creek and ridge where the paved path makes for easy walking. Here is how to plan a day around both, using the details published in the park guides.

Tilden for variety

Charles Lee Tilden Regional Park covers 1,468 acres in the Berkeley hills and is one of the district’s three oldest parks. The official description mentions a carousel ride, a picnic, a swim at Lake Anza and a stroll through the Botanic Garden, and the park also has the Little Farm. The guide lists no parking fee, and the park is open from 5 a.m. to 10 p.m.

Facilities include BBQ grills, drinking water, picnic areas, restrooms, swimming and camping. The map data shows 26 parking areas and 18 restrooms, so the park is rarely short of places to stop. About 17.7 miles of named trails are mapped inside it across 12 trails. The longest is Seaview Trail at 2.8 miles, which is also the one with the most up and down, roughly 1,307 feet of climbing and descending. If you only want a gentle outing with kids, skip Seaview and stay near the lake, farm and carousel instead.

Dogs are allowed on a leash in developed areas, so check the signs at trailheads and near the swimming area before you let a pup off the lead.

Wildcat Canyon for quiet

Right next door, Wildcat Canyon Regional Park is a Richmond park beside the Tilden Nature Area. It has trails for hiking, biking and horseback riding, and the official page describes a significant portion of the paved Nimitz Way, which runs out from Inspiration Point in Tilden. The guide shows 2,364 acres, no parking fee and the same 5 a.m. to 10 p.m. hours. Dogs follow the same rule: on a leash in developed areas.

There are 12 named trails adding up to about 15 miles. Wildcat Creek Trail is the longest at 2.7 miles, and Conlon Trail has the most elevation change, about 783 feet. The map data shows two parking areas, including the Alvarado Staging Area, plus three restrooms, four drinking fountains, a picnic area, a playground and horseshoes. With fewer parking areas, this is the park where arriving earlier makes more difference.

Putting the two together

A simple plan is to start at Tilden in the morning while the lots are easy, spend the middle of the day at the Little Farm or the lake, and finish with an easy out-and-back along Nimitz Way for ridge views. Because both parks are managed by the East Bay Regional Park District and the guides list the same hours, the schedule is simple.

A few practical reminders:

  • Carry water even though both parks have drinking fountains. They are not everywhere along the trails.
  • Bring layers. Hill weather around Berkeley can change between the lakeside and the ridge.
  • Leave early enough to be out by closing, which is 10 p.m. at both parks.
  • Fees and rules can change. Each guide links to the park district’s own page, so confirm there before you go.

Both parks are in the Bay Area section of the California county park guide, which also compares fees, dog rules, hours and facilities for parks in six other counties.
