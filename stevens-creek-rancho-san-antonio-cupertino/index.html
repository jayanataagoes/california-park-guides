Stevens Creek County Park and Rancho San Antonio: Cupertino's Two Very Different Park Days

Cupertino sits between two parks that look similar on a map but work differently on the ground. One charges a per-vehicle fee and welcomes dogs on leash. The other is run by a different agency and sits beside a preserve where dogs are not allowed. Knowing which is which saves a wasted drive.

Stevens Creek County Park

Stevens Creek County Park is a 1,066-acre Santa Clara County park built around the 87-acre Stevens Creek Reservoir. The county acquired the first 400-acre parcel on January 24, 1924, a purchase that started the park preservation movement in Santa Clara County. The park guide lists parking at $6 per vehicle, dogs on a leash, and day-use hours from 8 a.m. to sunset.

The reservoir is open to limited-power boats, sailboats, kayaks and flotation devices from mid-April to mid-October, and anglers fish for bass, catfish and crappie. Launch and inspection passes are sold through the county’s reservation website. For a picnic, the Lakeshore Picnic area is on the shoreline, and six shaded first-come, first-served picnic areas are spread through the park, three of them along the creek. Two group areas can be reserved.

There are more than nine miles of single-track and multi-use trails for hikers, mountain bikers and equestrians. The mapped data counts 12 named trails adding up to about 7.6 miles, with Tony Look / Stevens Creek Trail the longest at 2 miles and Lookout Trail the steepest at about 464 feet of up and down. Birders should bring binoculars, because more than 125 bird species have been recorded here. If you want to skip the entry fee, the Lookout Trail and Madrone trailhead at 14593 Stevens Canyon Road has free parking and restrooms.

Sunset matters because closing follows it: around 4:51 p.m. in mid-December and 8:29 p.m. in mid-June.

Rancho San Antonio County Park

Rancho San Antonio County Park is a 289-acre county park next to the much larger Rancho San Antonio Open Space Preserve, about 2,180 acres. Although the land belongs to Santa Clara County, the Midpeninsula Regional Open Space District manages it, so parking details and current information are posted on Midpen’s site rather than the county’s. The park is open year-round from 8 a.m. until 30 minutes after sunset, and the main entrance is at 22500 Cristo Rey Drive.

The county park has hiking, bicycling and equestrian trails that link to the preserve’s larger network. Bicycles must stay on designated trails and are not allowed west of Deer Hollow Farm. Horses are limited to the equestrian staging area and the Coyote Trail. There are stretching bars at the restroom parking area and at the equestrian staging area, plus picnicking and a model airfield.

The big rule to know: dogs are not allowed in the adjoining Rancho San Antonio Open Space Preserve, with service dog provisions applying. Check the manager’s rules for your destination before you bring a pet.

Which one should you pick?

If you are bringing a dog, a boat or a picnic crowd, Stevens Creek is the better fit. If you want a short, flat start to a longer preserve hike and you are traveling without a dog, Rancho San Antonio makes sense. The two are about 3.6 miles apart in a straight line, so you could do a morning at one and an afternoon at the other.

Fees and rules change, so confirm with the managing agency’s page linked from each guide before you go.
